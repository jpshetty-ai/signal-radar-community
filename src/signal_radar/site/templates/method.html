Method

Signal Radar reads newsletters, stores one record per news item, classifies each item with a language model and counts how often companies, products and topics are mentioned over time. Everything on these pages is computed from that record; nothing is typed in by hand.

Themes

Themes are found by counting, not by a model. A momentum theme starts from an entity whose mentions rose most against its own baseline and adds the entities most often mentioned with it. The lens theme is built only from items rated relevant to the reader. A model then writes the title and two sentences from the theme's items, and the card names that model.

Rising and fading

Mentions in the last {{ meta.theme_window }} days are compared with the average of the four {{ meta.theme_window }}-day windows before. Score = (current − baseline) / √(baseline + 1), so going from 10 to 30 mentions outranks going from 0 to 3.

Confidence: {{ conf('high') }} high means at least 8 items from both source families; {{ conf('medium') }} medium means at least 4; {{ conf('low') }} low is a hint, not a finding.

Relevance and reading list

Each item gets a relevance score from 0 to 3 for this reader: {{ meta.lens }}

The reading list ranks the last {{ meta.reading_days }} days by relevance, then by whether more than one independent source covered the story, the momentum of its entities{% if edition == 'console' %} and, for AlphaSignal, reader engagement{% endif %}. The same story from two editions is shown once.

Your + and − votes in the console teach the ranking once there are at least three: items about entities and categories you liked move up, those like items you dismissed move down, by at most 0.8 points, so relevance still decides. Each moved item says why. A − also hides that item.

Models

Classification and entity extraction run on a local model on this computer. Theme summaries and answers use the configured writing and reasoning models{% if meta.writer_models %}; this build used {{ meta.writer_models | join(', ') }}{% endif %}. The local model is cheap and private but makes mistakes: categories and entity types are sometimes wrong, and summaries can overstate. Every claim links to its source items so it can be checked.

What leaves this computer

The pipeline reads Gmail (read-only), the public tldr.tech archive{% if edition == 'console' %} (AlphaSignal and Bay Area Letters from the email only: their sites and tracked links are never fetched){% endif %}, and public RSS feeds (EU AI Act Newsletter, Sifted, Tech.eu, European Commission digital strategy). Questions asked in the console are answered locally unless a cloud model is configured. Built {{ meta.built_at[:16] | replace('T', ' ') }} UTC.
